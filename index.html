Our origin story

The Interior Structure of the Artifact

In “The Interior Structure of the Artifact,” Elaine Scarry’s The Body in Pain: The Making and Unmaking of the World considers how physical pain can resist language and make a person’s world feel smaller or disappear. Scarry also explores how imagination and making give ideas form in the world. Her account connects the difficulty of expressing pain with the ways people create and share meaning.

These ideas help us ask what pain means in Westworld, where hosts’ suffering, memories, and changing stories complicate the boundary between human and machine. If pain is difficult to communicate, how can we know whether another being is conscious? We’re exploring how the hosts’ experiences—and the worlds built around them—raise questions about consciousness, identity, and what it means to be heard.
